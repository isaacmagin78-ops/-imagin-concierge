# Module 2 — Decoding the Aid Letter

**The letter is designed to look generous. Your job is to find out what it actually costs.**

There is no required format for a financial aid offer. Every school writes its
own, uses its own words, and decides what to show and what to leave out. Two
letters can describe wildly different deals using nearly identical language.

That is not an accident. This module teaches you to take one apart in about
ten minutes.

---

## Step 1 — Sort every line into one of four buckets

Ignore the school's headings. Ignore the words "award" and "package." Go line
by line and sort.

### 🟢 Bucket 1 — Free money (grants and scholarships)

You keep it. You never repay it. This is the only bucket that reduces what
college costs you.

Words you'll see: *grant, scholarship, award, tuition remission, merit award,
Pell Grant, institutional grant.*

### 🔴 Bucket 2 — Student debt (federal loans)

Your kid borrows this and repays it with interest after graduation.

- **Direct Subsidized Loan** — need-based. The government covers the interest
  while your kid is enrolled at least half-time. This is the best loan on the
  page.
- **Direct Unsubsidized Loan** — not need-based. Interest starts accruing the
  day it's disbursed, including all four years of school.

Federal limits for a dependent undergraduate run roughly $5,500 the first year
and rise to $7,500 by junior and senior year. If a letter shows a much larger
"student loan," look closer — something else is in there.

### 🚨 Bucket 3 — Parent debt (Parent PLUS loans)

**This is the one that hurts, and it is the one most often disguised as aid.**

A Parent PLUS loan is *you* borrowing money. Not your kid. You are legally
responsible for repaying it. It carries a higher interest rate than your kid's
loans plus an origination fee taken off the top, and there is no meaningful
limit — a parent can borrow up to the full cost of attendance.

That last part is why it appears on so many letters. A school can advertise
that it "meets 100% of demonstrated need" by filling the gap with a loan you
take out. The need is met. The money is yours.

Words to watch for: *PLUS, Parent Loan, Direct PLUS, parent financing option.*

### 🟡 Bucket 4 — A job (work-study)

Federal Work-Study is not money on the bill. It's permission to hold a
part-time campus job, paid to your kid as a paycheck as they earn it, usually
in small amounts across the semester. If they work fewer hours, they get less.

**It does not reduce the bill in August.** Treat it as $0 when comparing offers.

---

## Step 2 — Run the only calculation that matters

> ### Cost of Attendance − 🟢 Free money = **What this school actually costs you**

Everything in buckets 2, 3, and 4 is how they propose you *pay* that number.
None of it makes the number smaller.

**Find the real Cost of Attendance.** Some letters show only tuition and fees.
The real number includes housing, meals, books, transportation, and personal
expenses. If the letter shows a suspiciously small cost, the missing pieces
didn't disappear — they're just not printed. Every school publishes a full
cost of attendance; ask for it in writing.

---

## Step 3 — Worked example

Two real-shaped offers. One says it meets 100% of need. Watch what happens.

### 🅰️ State University

| Line item | Amount | Bucket |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Cost of Attendance | $28,000 | — |
| University Grant | $8,000 | 🟢 Free |
| Direct Subsidized Loan | $3,500 | 🔴 Student debt |
| Direct Unsubsidized Loan | $2,000 | 🔴 Student debt |
| Federal Work-Study | $2,500 | 🟡 A job |
| **Letter says "Total Aid Package"** | **$16,000** | |

**Free money: $8,000.**
**Real cost: $28,000 − $8,000 = $20,000 per year.**

### 🅱️ Private College — *"We meet 100% of demonstrated need"*

| Line item | Amount | Bucket |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Cost of Attendance | $65,000 | — |
| Institutional Scholarship | $42,000 | 🟢 Free |
| Direct Subsidized Loan | $3,500 | 🔴 Student debt |
| Direct Unsubsidized Loan | $2,000 | 🔴 Student debt |
| **Parent PLUS Loan** | **$17,500** | 🚨 **Your debt** |
| **Letter says "Total Aid Package"** | **$65,000** | |

**Free money: $42,000.**
**Real cost: $65,000 − $42,000 = $23,000 per year.**

### The comparison

| | State U | Private College |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Headline "aid" | $16,000 | $65,000 |
| Actual free money | $8,000 | $42,000 |
| **What it costs you per year** | **$20,000** | **$23,000** |
| **Over four years** | **$80,000** | **$92,000** |
| Parent debt built in | $0 | $17,500/yr → **$70,000** |

The school offering **five times more "aid"** and claiming to meet 100% of
need is **$12,000 more expensive** over four years — and hands you $70,000 of
debt in your own name.

Both letters are accurate. Neither is lying. One is just easier to misread.

---

## Step 4 — Four things the letter won't tell you

**1. Multiply by four.** Every letter is one year. A $5,000 gap is a $20,000
gap. Ask: *"Is this scholarship renewable all four years, and what GPA does it
require?"* Front-loaded aid — generous freshman year, thinner after — is a
real practice.

**2. Costs rise every year.** Your aid usually doesn't rise with them. Ask
what tuition has increased annually for the last three years.

**3. Outside scholarships can backfire.** Some schools reduce their own grant
when your kid wins an outside scholarship. Ask: *"How do you treat outside
scholarships — do they reduce my grant or my loans?"* You want loans.

**4. A "$0 balance" can be all borrowed.** A letter showing nothing owed may
have gotten there entirely with PLUS loans.

## Step 5 — The comparison worksheet

Fill this out for every school. One page. Bring it to the kitchen table.

| | School 1 | School 2 | School 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full Cost of Attendance | | | |
| 🟢 Grants + scholarships | | | |
| **REAL COST (COA − grants)** | | | |
| × 4 years | | | |
| 🔴 Student loans offered | | | |
| 🚨 Parent PLUS offered | | | |
| Scholarship renewable? Y/N | | | |
| GPA required to keep it | | | |
| Cash we can pay per year | | | |
| **Gap we must cover** | | | |

**The bottom row is the decision.** Not the headline. Not the prestige. Not
which letter felt best to open.

---

## Step 6 — Call the financial aid office

They are used to these calls. Ask exactly this:

1. "Is this the **full** cost of attendance, including housing, meals, books,
   and travel?"
2. "Which items here are **loans**, and which are **grants**?"
3. "Is the Parent PLUS loan **required**, or is it a financing option you're
   showing me?"
4. "Is the scholarship **renewable** all four years? What GPA keeps it?"
5. "How do you handle **outside scholarships**?"
6. "If our circumstances changed, what's your process for a
   **special circumstances review**?" *(See Module 1 — this is the appeal.)*

Write the answers down with the date and the name of who told you.

---

## The whole module in five lines

1. Only grants and scholarships reduce what college costs.
2. **Cost of Attendance − free money = your real price.** Nothing else counts.
3. Parent PLUS is you borrowing, not the school giving.
4. Work-study is a job, not a discount.
5. Multiply everything by four.
