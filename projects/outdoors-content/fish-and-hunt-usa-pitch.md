## Talking points if they call

**"What exactly do you produce?"**
One brief becomes a full launch package. Proven output on a real client: 19 finished
assets from a single listing brief — cinematic film plan with shot list and voiceover,
three reels on three different buyer angles, a five-slide story sequence, launch and
recap posts, and a QR signage kit where each sign is tracked separately.

**"How fast?"**
Days. Rendering is programmatic, not a manual edit per asset — which is also why volume
doesn't multiply the price.

**"What do you need from us?"**
Brand colors, logo, the client's existing footage or photos, and a one-page brief. If
they have nothing shot, that's a separate conversation.

**"Why should we send you work instead of doing it ourselves?"**
You already do the high-value pieces — site, SEO, hero film, drone. This is the recurring
low-margin drip that eats a production calendar. It's additional revenue on clients you
already own, with no new headcount.

**On price:** don't quote a number cold. Ask what they currently charge for a video
package and what their clients ask for that they turn down. Price against that.

## Honest gaps to know before the call

- **No on-location or drone footage capability yet.** They have drone crews. Don't imply
  parity. The anthem film uses stock for three scenes.
- **No client base in this niche** beyond Legends Ranch.
- **One person.** If they ask about capacity, be straight and scope accordingly.

## Drone — the one gap worth closing

It's the cheapest capability gap to close and it's the thing that would make this a full
offer rather than a partial one.

**Requirement to know up front:** flying a drone for *any* commercial purpose in the US
requires an **FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate**. Recreational flying doesn't, but
the moment footage is used for a paying client it's commercial. It's a knowledge test at
an FAA-approved center (roughly $175, no flight test), plus registering the aircraft.

Do that before quoting drone work, not after. `VERIFY` current requirements at
faa.gov/uas before booking a test — rules in this area move.
