# Back Matter

## Back Cover Copy
Wesley Negbaum is the most misunderstood manatee in the whole wide sea.

The reef hears his name and decides he must be negative. Then the Salty Sourpusses join
the fray, spreading rumors, stirring the shoal, and trying to pull Wesley into their
favorite game: **Who's Got It Worse?**

But with help from Happy the Clam and Marcus A. Whaleus, Wesley learns that names do not
steer ships. **Choices do.**

Soon Wesley changes the game from blame to gratitude with **Good, Better, Best**, proving
that the same name they laughed at before can become the name they remember.

Because choices shape fate, not a name.
And a good captain steers through the blame.

## Core Moral
- **Child-facing:** You can't choose every thought that swims by, but you can choose which thoughts you feed.
- **Parent-facing:** Children may inherit patterns, labels, fears, and language from family or culture, but they are not trapped by them. With quiet, truth, gratitude, wise guidance, and good friends, they can notice negative self-talk and practice a better way.

## Parent / Teacher Discussion Questions
1. Why did the reef judge Wesley before knowing him?
2. What did the Salty Sourpusses want Wesley to believe?
3. What does "loose lips sink ships" mean in this story?
4. Why did Wesley start to believe the Sourpusses?
5. How did Happy help Wesley?
6. What did Marcus mean when he said, "The obstacle's the way"?
7. How did Wesley change the game?
8. What is one thing you are grateful for? Can you make it Good, Better, Best?
9. What does it mean to own your name?
10. What does "your choices determine the ship's course" mean?
11. Who helps you stay on the good current?
