Section 03

The honest read

You are competing in a category where the going rate is $500 to $1,000 a month, and you have been delivering above that standard for nothing. That is the single biggest gap between where you are and where the market already is.

The market is also splitting. Analysts expect consolidation through 2030, with a widening gap between digital-first mass-premium providers and human-led boutique operators. Almost every boutique concierge is a digital laggard, and almost every digital-first provider has no human taste. You are sitting in the one corner nobody occupies well: boutique judgment delivered at software speed. That is not a positioning exercise — it is literally what you did tonight, twice.

Second finding, and it is the uncomfortable one: travel is 36–41% of the entire luxury concierge market — the biggest and fastest-growing slice of the business you are already in. It is also the idea you have been sitting on longest. That is worth revisiting on its own merits, separately from any question of who you build it with.

Third: no single tool wins all four agent workflows — capture, nurture, listing prep, transaction. The software is stuck fighting over capture and nurture. Listing prep is where your reels live, and almost nobody is serving it well.

Section 04

The five that count

Put a price on it this month

Anchor at $750/month for a named client, which sits inside the band agents already pay Structurely and Ylopo without blinking. Not a discount, not a trial — a number.

Free work has no comparison price, so it gets valued at nothing.

Sell the artifact, not the assistant

"AI executive concierge" is abstract and every competitor says it. A reel, a company brief, a listing package — those are objects a buyer can hold up. Lead with the thing you hand them.

Scott asked "name a company" after seeing one object, not one pitch.

Make Linda a case study, not a favor

One named client with a stated result — listing, reel, open house, outcome — is the only sales asset that closes the next five. Ask permission to publish the result before you deliver it.

A pilot with no published outcome is charity with extra steps.

One landing page, one QR, on everything

Every brief, reel end-card, deck, and sign carries the same code to the same page. The brief already travels — right now it travels without a way back to you.

You built the QR mechanism tonight for the gate. It is the same mechanism.

Take the travel segment seriously

It is 36–41% of your own market and the largest growth area in it. Whatever the history there, the market fact stands independently and deserves a decision rather than a drift.

The biggest slice of the category you are already selling into.

Section 05

The full thirty
Positioning 01–05
  1. 01Claim the boutique-plus-digital corner explicitly. Say the sentence out loud in the deck: human judgment, software speed. ★ TOP 5
  2. 02Drop "AI" from the lead line. Every competitor says AI. Nobody else can hand over what you make.
  3. 03Name the buyer precisely — top-producing agents at brokerages with no in-house marketing.
  4. 04Pick one vertical to win first. Luxury residential in South Florida is small enough to dominate and rich enough to matter.
  5. 05Write a one-sentence promise you can repeat identically for a year.
Pricing & packaging 06–11
  1. 06Set $750/month as the anchor. Inside the band the market already pays. ★ TOP 5
  2. 07Build three tiers, not one price. The market moved to tiered membership; single pricing reads amateur.
  3. 08Sell an annual, not a monthly. $2,000–$8,000 a year is the established shape of this category.
  4. 09Price the one-off brief separately so a buyer can start without a contract.
  5. 10Publish the price. Concealed pricing makes a boutique look like it is guessing per client.
  6. 11Charge for the pilot, even a token amount. A paid pilot converts; a free one ends.
Product 12–17
  1. 12Lead with the artifact. The brief and the reel are the product. ★ TOP 5
  2. 13Own listing prep. The one agent workflow the AI tools have not taken.
  3. 14Standardise the reel the way the company brief is now standardised — one spec, any listing.
  4. 15Ship a monthly cadence, not one-offs. Recurring output is what justifies recurring price.
  5. 16White-label it. The client's name at the top is the whole product for advisors and agents.
  6. 17Keep the compliance rails visible. The NAR guardrail already written is a selling point, not overhead.
Distribution & proof 18–24
  1. 18One landing page, one QR, on every artifact. ★ TOP 5
  2. 19Publish Linda's result as a case study. ★ TOP 5
  3. 20Put the QR on the reel end-card, where it sits on screen for three seconds anyway.
  4. 21Send one brief a week to a named person who did not ask. Cheapest possible demand test.
  5. 22Track who scans. A landing page with no analytics repeats tonight's attribution problem.
  6. 23Ask every recipient for one referral at the moment they compliment the work, not later.
  7. 24Credit collaborators publicly — photographers, agents. It costs nothing and buys the next introduction.
Operations & leverage 25–30
  1. 25Decide the travel question. 36–41% of the market. ★ TOP 5
  2. 26One tool, one job. Research gathers, the assistant formats, one place holds the truth.
  3. 27Keep the brand fixed. The serif and the monogram are equity now; change the world around them, not them.
  4. 28Kill the recurring jobs that generate work rather than finish it. A daily briefing that manufactures a nine-item list is a cost.
  5. 29Audit the subscriptions. Linktree Pro has billed since July with nothing in it; the same pattern will repeat elsewhere.
  6. 30Fix the broken checkout before adding anything new. A live product that cannot take a card outranks every idea on this page.
