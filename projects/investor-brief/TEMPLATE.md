# IMAGIN CONCIERGE — Company Trajectory Brief

Reusable spec for the one-pager format proven on the Lucid Group (LCID) brief.
This codifies a design Isaac already made. It is not a redesign — it exists so the
second, fifth, and twentieth brief take minutes instead of a night.

Colors and type below are read off a photo of the finished piece, so treat them as
close-but-approximate until confirmed against the source file.

---

## Why the format works

It isn't a data dump. It's an argument with a shape:

> a company earned a reputation → something forced a correction → here is the
> specific test it now faces → here is the honest bottom line

Anyone can list financials. The arc plus a stated judgment is what reads as research
rather than as a graphic. **Preserve the arc. Everything else is decoration.**

---

## Slot skeleton

| Slot | Content | Length |
|---|---|---|
| `PREPARED_FOR` | "Prepared for [initials/name]" — top right, small | ≤ 20 chars |
| `COMPANY` | Legal name + ticker, e.g. `LUCID GROUP (LCID)` | ≤ 28 chars |
| `SPAN` | Era the brief covers, e.g. `2024–2026 TRAJECTORY` | ≤ 24 chars |
| `KICKER` | Fixed: `MARKET PERSPECTIVE` | — |
| `ACT_1` | Label + 1 line, e.g. `2021–2023 \| PROMISE MEETS EXECUTION` | 2 sentences |
| `ACT_2` | The turning point, e.g. `LATE 2023 \| RESET` | 1–2 sentences |
| `ACT_3` | The current test, e.g. `2024–2026 \| THE PROVING GROUND` | label only |
| `WATCH[4]` | Exactly four. One word + one short phrase each | ≤ 45 chars each |
| `HERO` | Product photograph, cinematic, bottom third | — |
| `BOTTOM_LINE` | 2 sentences. Opportunity, then the condition on it | ≤ 200 chars |

### Fill rules

- **Three acts, never four.** Four is a timeline; three is a story.
- **Act labels are a period plus a two-or-three word verdict** — `PROMISE MEETS
  EXECUTION`, `RESET`, `THE PROVING GROUND`. Name the chapter, don't summarize it.
- **Exactly four watch items.** Each is one capitalized noun plus a phrase. On Lucid:
  PRODUCTION / GRAVITY SUV / CAPITAL / DEMAND — one operational, one product, one
  financial, one market. Keep that spread; it's what makes the set feel complete.
- **The bottom line must contain a condition, not a prediction.** "Technology created
  the opportunity. Execution will determine whether it becomes a sustainable business."
  That sentence is defensible in a way "LCID is a buy" never is — and it's the reason
  the whole piece reads as credible.

## Design tokens

| Token | Value (approximate) |
|---|---|
| Background | near-black, ~`#0A0A0C` |
| Primary accent | warm gold, ~`#D4B36A` — headings, icons, rules |
| Body text | warm off-white, ~`#EDE7DA` |
| Display face | high-contrast serif, letterspaced, all-caps company name |
| Body face | clean sans, regular weight |
| Icons | thin-line, circular, gold, one per act |
| Background motif | technical blueprint line-art, low opacity, right edge |
| Hero | product photo, cinematic light, bleeds into the background |
| Bottom line | boxed with a thin gold rule, icon at left |

The blueprint motif is doing real work — it signals engineering scrutiny without
saying anything. Swap it per sector: chassis/drivetrain for autos, board traces for
semis, floorplates for real estate, molecular for pharma.

## Three things to add before this leaves a friend group

Small, and they're the difference between a nice image and a product.

1. **An "as of" date.** Every brief states the date its facts were current. Undated
   research reads as amateur and quietly goes wrong as it ages.
2. **A disclaimer line.** Small, bottom edge: informational and educational, not
   investment advice, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
3. **Sourcing for hard claims.** "Heavy cash burn" is fine among friends. A stranger
   asks where it came from. A small sources line, or footnote markers on the specific
   claims, closes that gap.

### One wording note

The Lucid brief says **"Prepared for BB."** It looks great and it raises perceived
value. It also makes the piece read as personalized advice for one person rather than
as general research — and that distinction is exactly the one that separates a
publishing business from a regulated advisory one. Keeping the courtesy line is fine;
just make sure the analysis itself is written for any reader, and the disclaimer is
present. Content impersonal, presentation personal.

## Producing the next one

1. Name the company and the era the brief covers.
2. Write the three acts first, in plain sentences, before touching design. If the arc
   doesn't hold in plain text, the layout won't save it.
3. Pick the four watch items across the four axes: operational, product, financial,
   market.
4. Write the bottom line as opportunity + condition.
5. Lay it into the format above. Date it. Disclaim it.

Step 2 is the whole job. Steps 3–5 are assembly.
