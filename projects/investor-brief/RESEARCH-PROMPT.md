# Research prompt — feeding the brief

Use a search-grounded tool that cites sources (Perplexity is well suited). The point
is not to get an essay back. It is to get the **slots in `TEMPLATE.md`, already
sourced**, so the writing step is editing rather than starting.

Run the research prompt first. Only then write the brief.

---

## The prompt (any company)

Replace `[COMPANY]` and `[SPAN]`. Paste as-is.

> Research **[COMPANY]** and return findings in the exact structure below. Cite a
> source for every factual claim, with the date the fact was reported. Where you
> cannot source something, write "unsourced" rather than estimating — I would rather
> have a gap than a number I have to defend.
>
> **1. ACT ONE — the reputation.** What did this company become known for, and in
> what period? Two sentences of substance, plus the specific achievements that earned
> the reputation.
>
> **2. ACT TWO — the correction.** What forced a change in priorities, and roughly
> when? Delays, cash burn, a failed product, a market shift, a leadership change.
> Give me the turning point, not a list of setbacks.
>
> **3. ACT THREE — the current test.** For the period **[SPAN]**, what does this
> company actually have to prove? Give exactly four items, one from each category:
> - operational (can it execute at scale)
> - product (what expansion or launch is it betting on)
> - financial (cash position, funding, cost discipline)
> - market (is the demand durable)
>
> For each: one capitalized keyword, one short phrase, and the evidence behind it.
>
> **4. BOTTOM LINE.** In two sentences: what opportunity exists, and what specific
> condition determines whether it is realized. State a condition, not a prediction.
> Do not tell me whether to invest.
>
> **5. CONTRADICTIONS.** Where do credible sources disagree about this company? List
> the disputes plainly — I need to know what is contested before I put my name on it.
>
> **6. AS OF.** The date through which your information is current, and anything
> material you know is pending or unresolved.

## Extra clauses for a private company

SpaceX, Stripe, Anduril and the like have no ticker, no filings, no quarterly
numbers. Append this:

> This company is **private**. There are no public filings, so be explicit about
> provenance on everything financial:
> - Distinguish a **reported valuation** (a specific funding round or tender offer,
>   with date and lead investor) from a **rumored** or **secondary-market** figure.
>   Secondary-market prices circulate widely and go stale fast — label them.
> - Revenue and subscriber figures for private companies are usually leaked, briefed
>   to reporters, or estimated by third parties. Say which, every time.
> - Do not carry forward a valuation from an older round as if it were current.
> - Where a public competitor gives a useful reference point, say so explicitly
>   rather than blending it in.
>
> If a number is widely repeated but traces back to a single unnamed source, tell me
> that. One anonymous claim repeated by twenty outlets is still one claim.

---

## Then, before writing

Three checks. They take a minute and they are the whole difference between a brief
that holds and one that doesn't.

1. **Does the arc survive in plain text?** Write the three acts as bare sentences,
   no design. If it isn't a story that way, the layout will not rescue it.
2. **Is the bottom line a condition?** "X will determine whether Y" — defensible.
   "This is a buy" — not, and not the business we're in.
3. **Is anything in the brief traceable to a single anonymous source?** If yes,
   either cut it or say it's contested.

---

## What the brief carries

Every one, without exception:

- an **as-of date**
- a **disclaimer line** — informational and educational, not investment advice, not a
  recommendation to buy or sell any security
- **sourcing** for the hard claims

For a private company, add one line noting that figures are drawn from reported and
secondary-market sources rather than public filings. That sentence costs nothing and
answers the first question any serious reader will have.
