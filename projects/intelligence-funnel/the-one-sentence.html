The one sentence

The research was blunt: the most common reason a business like this dies is that the owner can't say what it is in one sentence. You don't have to write it. Just find which one is already yours.

The actual test

Not which reads best. Which one could you say out loud to Arturo without feeling stupid — and which one would he repeat back correctly to somebody else the next day?

If it were mine to pick

"I get you in, and I tell you exactly what you're walking into."

Number 4's structure, in number 2's voice. Access and judgment together — which is what the evidence supports — but short enough to survive being repeated, and it sounds like you rather than a brochure.

Travel isn't in the sentence on purpose. It's layer three, it's where this ends up, and it's the part with a licensing question still open. It gets added the day it's real, not before.

What happens once you pick one

Everything downstream stops being a guess. What the free front door has to prove in ninety seconds. What a membership includes and — more importantly — what it doesn't. What you say to the first person you try this on.

And you don't have to take any of mine. Say it your own way and that's the real one. Yours beats mine every time, because you're the one who has to say it out loud.
